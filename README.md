# CryptoCrimeEvolution

**Evolution of the Criminal Use of Cryptoassets, 2011–2026**

A longitudinal study of documentary evidence on how cryptoassets are acquired, transferred, concealed, converted, and used.

**Author — Dmytro Lande**

## Research focus

How do the documented roles of cryptoassets, technologies, and intermediaries change over time, and how can these changes be distinguished from changes in information coverage?

## Data

- Seven InfoStream search exports containing online media reports, institutional releases, and other open-source materials.
- Coverage from **21 April 2011 to 29 September 2026**.
- **6,469 records**, with **5,206 retained** after thematic screening.
- **4,678 text families** grouping identical or closely similar publications to reduce the influence of republication.
- **234 analytical cards** describing cases, processes, and practices.

## Approach

1. Adaptive information retrieval with preserved query versions.
2. Thematic screening and grouping of similar texts.
3. Concept extraction and reconstruction of operations and participant roles.
4. Retrieval of supporting passages and LLM-assisted analysis under researcher supervision.
5. Programmatic measurement of six indicator groups and comparison across time windows.

Agent functions cover screening, concept extraction, episode reconstruction, temporal alignment, evidence verification, and summary generation. Retrieval augmentation supports passage-based analysis; RAG performance was not independently evaluated.

## Concepts examined

- Asset mixing and traceability obstruction.
- Cross-chain transfers.
- Stablecoins, including USDT.
- Decentralized finance (DeFi) and smart contracts.
- Fiat conversion, bank transfers, and cash withdrawals.
- Know Your Customer (KYC) and anti-money laundering (AML).

## Main findings

Shares of text-family representatives containing the corresponding lexical indicators:

| Indicator | 2023–2024 | 2025–2026 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Mixing | 36.8% | 21.9% |
| Stablecoins | 15.0% | 34.6% |
| KYC/AML | 29.7% | 43.4% |

- Topic profiles change non-monotonically. These three directions of change also persist when analysis is restricted to core thematic materials.
- Case analysis identifies specialized conversion intermediaries and combinations of blockchain, banking, and cash infrastructure.
- Asset swaps, cross-chain transfers, bank payouts, cash withdrawals, and direct payments are treated as distinct operational outcomes.
- Event time is distinguished from reporting time. A later procedural development in an existing case is not counted as a new criminal episode.
- A discrete framework separates observed corpus indicators from underlying practices and changes in their documentary visibility.

## Interpretation limits

The indicators measure **mentions within the selected corpus**, not crime prevalence or illicit transaction volumes. Queries and source coverage changed over time, and 2026 is incomplete. Text families are not criminal cases; analytical cards are not a count of independent crimes. Mentioning a technology does not establish its criminal use. Numerical forecasting and causal effects of enforcement measures were not estimated.

## Repository purpose

Document the research, preserve traceable source references, support reproduction of descriptive calculations, and enable further longitudinal analysis.

Intended audiences include researchers in OSINT, financial forensics, and criminology.
